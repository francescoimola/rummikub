# Francesco Imola's portfolio

The words for the parts of francescoimola.com: a design studio's portfolio, writing and art, by one person.

## Work

**Case study**:
A client project with its own page, listed on the homepage.
_Avoid_: Work page, portfolio item

**Explorations**:
Self-initiated or experimental work with no client attached, shown as images in the homepage carousel. Explorations have no pages.
_Avoid_: Side projects, experiments

**Artmaking**:
Francesco's art practice and its projects, shown on `/art/`.
_Avoid_: Art projects page, gallery

## Writing

**Ideas**:
The writing section of the site, named "Ideas" in the navigation. Its page title is "Ideas & Guides" and its hero reads "Ideas and Guides".
_Avoid_: Blog, Ideas & guides as the navigation label (old label)

## Layout

**Content light**:
The white-tinted background behind page content (`--content-light`), set per page with `contentMode: contrast`. Figma draws it on every page.
_Avoid_: Contrast mode, white content

**Tight**:
The smallest vertical gap: between things that belong together, such as a heading and its own text, text and the link or button under it, list items, and images inside a pair or grid.
_Avoid_: Small gap, heading gap

**Flow**:
The normal vertical gap between paragraphs, and between a paragraph and a list or divider. Grows with screen width.
_Avoid_: Paragraph gap, text gap

**Break**:
The vertical gap where a new topic starts: above a heading inside a section or long-form page, and around images, image pairs and image grids.
_Avoid_: Heading gap, media gap

**Chapter**:
The largest vertical gap: between sections, and between the page title and the hero image. Named Chapter, not Section, because Section already means the label | text row.
_Avoid_: Section gap

**Hang**:
The very small gap where a heading hangs from something: under a section stroke, or between a card's heading and its text. A deliberate exception, smaller than the four vertical gaps.
_Avoid_: Stroke gap, heading gap

**Section stroke**:
A heavy or light line placed before an h2 or h3 to mark where a new section begins; the heading hangs from it.
_Avoid_: Divider, hr (an accordion's thin line between questions is a divider, not a section stroke)

**Illustration band**:
A wide illustration set on its own between two sections, with space built into the band itself rather than added as a margin on the drawing.
_Avoid_: Illustration margin
