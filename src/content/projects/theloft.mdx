The Loft is a multipurpose space in Folkestone with an eclectic audience and a growing range of offerings, including movement classes, studio and corporate hire, gigs, and workshops. Less than a year from launch, however, it became clear that their website, the endpoint of all their paid and organic marketing, wasn't serving its users due to poor UX. So Cairo and Nathan, founders of The Loft, hired me to help.

## Problem

The initial user research that I conducted showed us that The Loft had two main problems: an ambiguous messaging and a gap between understanding the offerings and taking action.

Messaging-wise, the site was failing at addressing visitors' pain points and offering clear solutions. Pages were thin where they needed depth, or front-loaded with disclaimers in places that should have been quick to move past.

The said gap showed up as a conversion problem at the narrowest part of their funnel. In practice, even if visitors found their way to the "correct" part of the site, they would be dropped into booking flows before they knew what they were booking.

## Approach

I developed a StoryBrand redesign that flipped the job of the website from transactional to nurturing, with the visitor as the hero of their story—a hero with a problem—and the brand as the guide that offers them a clear path to follow.

First, I introduced a more restrained navigation to make the website easier to browse. Once you're on the homepage, you're presented with three possible paths: Ritual (their movement studio), Events, and Hire. The exception is if you're looking for their location, which is easily accessible through a link in the header.

Everything else, including pricing, social proof and details about each offering, I folded inside the structure of each page. For example, the page for Ritual is made of nine sections that help build a mood and offer engaged users meaningful content to not only answer every possible question but also explain how Ritual at The Loft stacks against local competition—the "why here and not somewhere else?" element that is so important to conversions.

Following a similar approach, the rest of the pages on this site walk the customer from "I have questions" (at the top) to "I'm at peace and I know what to do next" (at the bottom).

## Solution

The result is a four-page website, consolidated from fourteen. The full scope of the offer is still there, but we're telling a far more compelling story, and we're gently guiding users to book or enquire directly. Without forcing their hand and without leading them down rabbit holes.

What's more, once I identified what the strenghts of The Loft were, I focused on showcasing said strengths, instead of dwelling on any perceived drawbacks, which is what the previous site tended to do. For instance, location-wise, The Loft is on the top floor of an old department store, right in the heart of Folkestone high street. Admittedly, this means there are no lifts, and guests really need to read the access instructions to find the venue. In return, its location enables them to host movement classes and events with considerably louder music than most independent studios in town. Another advantage is that no other space in this part of town can accommodate such a variety of purposes so effortlessly, from sunny photoshoots during the day to film screenings at night — both of which are only possible due to The Loft's particular size, orientation, and aboveground location.

Last but not least, a redesign is of no use if the client can't easily take care of their swanky new website. Which is why I consulted closely with Cairo throughout the entire process. By the end it, he was familiar with the reasoning behind each element on each page. And before I handed over the keys to the website, I also provided extensive documentation and training videos.
